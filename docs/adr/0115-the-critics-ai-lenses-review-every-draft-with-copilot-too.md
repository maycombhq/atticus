# 0115. The critic's AI lenses review every draft, with Copilot too

- Status: accepted (pending review by the product owner)
- Date: 2026-10-23
- Builds on: ADR 0074 (AI → deterministic → AI), 0086 (`--handoff`), 0087 (the stages as loops), 0090 (the VS Code runner and `--handoff --json`), 0106 addendum (the critic's lenses handed off one at a time), 0113 (the fast path)

## Context

Each stage (feature, architecture, stories, and the small-change fast path) drafts through the AI runtime, checks the draft with its own rules and runs a critic round on every draft (ADR 0087). With a model runtime the round runs the critic's AI lenses too. In hand-off mode, which is how the VS Code extension runs every AI step with Copilot (`atticus <step> --handoff --json`, one prompt per round), the round ran its rules only. Its lenses were recorded as not run (`lenses: []` in `review/<gate>/<n>/meta/round.yaml`) with a note sending the person to `/atticus-critic`. So with Copilot no model ever reviewed a draft. The scan's second AI pass (rule 4) was missing from the stages.

A live run on a real metarepo showed what that costs. A feature placement for "mute a room" marked the post-message flow as only read, although nothing in the estate sends notifications. No rule can catch that. It takes a reading of the draft against the estate and the brief, which is the architecture or business lens's job.

The lenses also didn't see the draft. The critic reads the change folder's artifacts (brief, proposal, design and so on). `feature.md`, `feature.yaml` and `target-flows/` aren't artifacts, so a define-gate lens reviewed the brief and not the placement drafted from it.

## Decision

1. **Hand-off lenses are further hand-offs of the stage.** The stage loop gives the critic the stage's own runtime for its lenses, hand-off included (`criticRuntime: null` still means rules only). When the round's lenses go to the person's assistant, the stage's step ends in state `waiting` on the first lens's run, with its prompt and result file (`findings-<lens>.yaml` in the run's folder). After `atticus handoff done <run>`, which reads that lens into the round as it does for `atticus critic`, the stage's resume offers the next lens still out, oldest first. When every lens is in, the round's open blocking findings join what the stage's checks and the critic's rules found in that draft. They then follow the loop's usual rules: a blocking finding goes back with the next draft, a repeat or a flip becomes a question (ADR 0087), and the critic's knowledge gaps become questions as they do now. The loop keeps the waiting draft in `stage/<stage>.yaml` (`review`: the gate, round, the draft's run and files, and what it had found), so a resume picks up where it was. A lens answer that `handoff done` validated but didn't read in is read in by the resume.
2. **The lenses review the draft against the estate and the brief.** The critic takes the stage's draft (`review` in `runCritic`): the files the stage wrote (`feature.md`, `feature.yaml`, `target-flows/<flow>.yaml`, and the artifacts it writes) join each lens's documents, which it may cite as it cites the artifacts, and the estate the drafter read joins as data, below the artifacts. Each lens prompt says it reviews the draft against the estate and the brief, including what the draft left out. A finding must still name a rule the lens was given and quote the line it cites (ADR 0111). `completeLensRun` reads a draft file it cites from the change folder.
3. **Bounded as before.** Which lenses run at which gate comes from the org pack: the gate's checklists, the change type's lenses and each rule's artifacts. A gate with no AI rule offers no lens. The stage's loop rules still apply, and so do the gate's round limit and the AI-off setting (`runtime.yaml ai: off` records the lenses as not run). Lenses run only on a draft that passed the stage's checks. A draft with a blocking finding of its own goes back to the assistant first, with its lenses recorded as skipped.
4. **The protocol.** `--handoff --json` adds `lens` to a hand-off that is one of the critic's lenses. For the stage steps, as for `critic`, a resume whose run was read in offers the change's next waiting lens run. The VS Code runner follows `resumeArgs` as before. A lens's first offer doesn't count toward its hand-off limit (eight by default), because the limit bounds drafts and each draft brings a lens or two. A lens offered again (its answer didn't pass) does count, so a lens can't loop for ever. Without `--json`, the stage says which lens it handed off.

## Consequences

- With Copilot, every draft that passes the rules is reviewed by a model before the next draft or the stage's PR. The loop is AI → deterministic → AI again.
- Each lens is one more prompt per draft. With the default feature type and an org pack with AI rules at every lens, that is up to six. Each lens prompt carries the draft and the estate, so it is bigger than a critic lens's prompt on artifacts alone.
- No contract changes: the C3 finding and the round meta keep their shapes (a lens's `artifacts` may now name a draft file), C5 is unchanged, and `stage/<stage>.yaml` gains an optional field (no version bump). The `--handoff --json` output gains an optional field.
- Not built: a lens that judges a draft without an AI rule in the org pack (the built-in constitution has only deterministic rules, so a pack with no AI rule gets no lens review; changed by ADR 0120: every new metarepo gets a starter review of AI rules), and the IntelliJ plugin, which runs no hand-off loop of its own.

## Addendum (2026-10-23): what an adversarial review found

An adversarial review of the loop found these failures. The fixes keep the decision above. They make each failure fail closed, and they carry the step's facts as data.

1. **A lens that didn't review is not a review.** A lens answer without a `findings` list (empty, a refusal, prose) fails `handoff done` (`yamlAnswerProblems`), and is offered again. In model mode `parseLensOutput` returns `malformed` and the lens is recorded `failed`. A failed lens in the newest round of a gate is a hand-off reason (`lens-failed`), as a waiting one is. A lens whose run is gone or was cancelled is recorded `failed` (`failLens`).
2. **The stage stops instead of reporting done** when a lens of the draft's round didn't review, or when the gate's round limit leaves the draft without a critic round. Running the stage again from a stop re-checks the draft on file and opens a new critic round on it. The stage's own blocking findings go back before any critic round, so a round is used only on a draft worth reviewing.
3. **The draft's own round.** `loop.review` is dropped whenever a fresh draft starts. A resume must name a lens of that round, or this stage's own draft (`request.change` and `outputPath`). Anything else is refused. `waitingLenses` takes the round.
4. **Ids, not words.** The loop links a finding to the question it became by id (`raiseQuestions`' `of`, ADR 0117). ADR 0117's judge decides which questions are the same. A finding answered and back in a draft that was given the answer stops the loop. `--handoff --json` offers the run the step reports through `Output.report` (`{ handoff: { runId, lens } }`), filtered to the step's change. A batched scan reports `pagesLeft`, and the end JSON carries it.
5. **Data, not instructions.** The last draft's findings and the answers people gave go in an untrusted `hand-backs` source. The trusted instructions only point to it.
6. **The text a lens saw.** The draft is copied into the lens run's folder (`draft/`) at the hand-off, and `completeLensRun` checks quotes against that copy.
7. **Checked again once merged.** `StageSpec.check(answer, { onFile })`: a done stage's result is checked again with `onFile`. A migration the catalog now has under the same file name, and a table or entry the delta marked new, are the change's own.
8. **Lenses run headless too, read-only.** Failing closed made a lens that may not run on headless Claude Code stop every headless stage. The built-in lens agents now allow `headless-claude-code`, and a reviewer (an agent whose output is findings, kept in its run) runs in its run folder whatever the step's invocation says, so with `--permission-prompts none` the only file it can write is its findings, never the draft. An org pack's own lens agents keep the runtimes they list.
9. **A round no lens reviewed uses up no round.** A round in which every AI lens it was due failed (Claude Code missing, a timeout, a provider outage) doesn't count toward `maxAuthorRounds` (`countedRounds`). Its layer-1 findings run again identically and for free. Before this, two failed starts used up a gate's two rounds and left the change with a person, though nothing was reviewed (found on a real metarepo).

No contract shape changes: `ReasonCode` gains `lens-failed`, and the `--handoff --json` end object may carry `pagesLeft`.

## Addendum (2026-10-24): a fix applied with no critic round left, and a stage ahead of a stuck gate

A live run on a real metarepo (Claude Code headless) found two more.

1. **A checking round past the limit, once a person acted.** At a design gate with `maxAuthorRounds: 2`, round 2 had one real blocking finding. A person fixed the target flow by hand and ran `atticus finding <id> apply`, which said "the next critic round checks the fix". No round was left, so the stage still said a person decides, and `waive` refused (the finding was already applied). Now, when a person has acted on every blocking finding of the gate's newest counted round (applied a fix, waived it with a reason, or dismissed it as not relevant, C3's actions as before) and on at least one in that round itself, not carried from the round before, the gate allows **one checking round past its limit** (`checkRoundDue`). The round's `meta/round.yaml` records `checkRound: { after, actedOn }`, the findings that allowed it. If it raises a blocking finding, a person decides again: the stage stops at once rather than draft over the person's fix, and a further round needs a person to act on that round's findings. So the rounds stay bounded by people's decisions, and nothing is waived silently: a waiver is still a person's, with a reason, carried by fingerprint. `atticus finding … apply` (and waive, not-relevant) says what really comes next when no round is left: the other blocking findings of the round still to act on, or the checking round and the command for it. `gateStatus` gains `checkRound`; `runCritic`'s result gains `checkRound` for a round past the limit; `atticus critic` and `atticus findings --json` show both. A round in which no lens reviewed still counts for nothing (point 9).
2. **A later stage waits for the earlier stage.** The stories stage ran while the design gate was stuck. Each stage reads what the one before wrote (ADR 0087: the architecture stage reads `feature.yaml`, the stories stage the target flows), and a stage is done only when nothing blocking is open, so a stage now refuses while an earlier stage of the path has run and isn't finished: its loop isn't `done`, or its gate has an open blocking finding, a lens still out or a lens that didn't review. The refusal names the stage, its gate and the command to finish it. A knowledge gap waiting for its owner doesn't hold the next stage up. A stage may still run before the earlier phase's hand-off PR merges (it says so, as before), and a merged hand-off means a person approved that gate. The order comes from the change policy's `stages` (ADR 0087 addendum).

No contract shape changes: `GateStatus.checkRound` and the round meta's `checkRound` are additions.

## Addendum (2026-10-24): a person's answers are part of the brief, and a checking round after a clean one

A live run on a real metarepo (headless Claude Code) found the critic blocking a decision a person had made, and a gate the person couldn't get past. The change was a chat feature; the fixtures retell it on the Forge estate as "pin an order note" (`fixtures/stages/pin-note/`).

1. **The lenses read the answers people gave.** A person answered "Which notes can be pinned?" with "text notes only". The business lens then blocked the draft: "the draft narrows the brief's 'pin one note' to text notes only". The lens prompt carried the brief, the draft and what the drafter read of the estate, but not the answers, so it judged the person's narrowing as a gap. (The stage passed its own sources on, which hold no answers, and dropped instruction sources; the hand-backs block that carries the answers to the drafter never reached the critic. The critic run on its own, `atticus critic`, read no questions at all.) Now the critic reads the change's answered questions itself (`changeAnswers`: the questions filed under the change, and those its stage loops raised), so both the stage's round and `atticus critic` see them. They go in an untrusted `answers-on-file` data block, each with its id; the trusted instructions tell the lens that the answers are part of the brief, that a scope, limit or choice a person set by answering is not a finding, and that a finding still about what an answer decided names it: `answered: { id, quote }`.
2. **A finding about a person's decision doesn't block; it is linked to the decision.** The link is checked as facts: the id is one of the answers the lens was given, and the quote is word for word in that answer (`checkedAnswerLink`); a link that fails is dropped and the finding stands as the rule says. A linked finding of a rule a person may waive is kept as a **risk**, with `answered: { question, quote }` (C3, additive). Why not block: the person already made the decision a waiver records, with their name and time, on the question; blocking again asks them to make it twice, and the loop would turn a repeat into a third question about the same thing. Why keep it: it is the lens's reading, and the PR's reviewer sees it beside the answer it is about. A constitution rule, or a rule that says it can't be waived, still blocks with the link shown: an answer is no stronger than a waiver. The lens says which answer (its own reading, batched into the prompt it sends anyway, as ADR 0117 §3 prefers); no word matching decides it. Not built: a judge kind that links a finding the lens didn't link.
3. **A checking round after a clean one.** At a gate with `maxAuthorRounds: 2`, round 3 was the checking round: no blocking finding, one gap. The person answered the draft's new question and wrote the gap's fact in by hand (`edit-and-apply`); the stage drafted again and stopped with "act on each blocking finding of round 3", which had none, so draft 4 sat unreviewed and the only way out was returning the change. A checking round is now due when **nothing blocking from the gate's newest counted round is still open** (it never had any, or a person acted on every one) **and a person acted since that round ran**: acted on any of its findings, a gap included (not carried from the round before), or answered a question raised for the change, timestamped after the round (`checkRoundState`). It stays bounded: each further round needs a new decision by a person. The refusal never tells a person to act on blocking findings there aren't: with none open it says nobody has acted since the round, and that answering the change's questions or acting on one of its findings makes a round due, or the change can be returned. `checkRound.actedOn` lists the findings and questions acted on.
4. **The used rounds in words.** After the checking round ran, the gate said "its 2 critic rounds are used", with round 3 on file. Once a checking round has run it says "its 2 critic rounds and the checking round are used" (`roundsUsed`, `GateStatus.checkRoundsRun`), in `atticus critic`, `atticus findings` and `atticus change status`, which now prints the line for a gate whose rounds are used up (`critic` in its JSON).
5. **A gap can't be applied; a person who wrote it in records that.** `atticus finding <change> <id> apply` on a knowledge gap says there is no fix to apply, and that a person who already wrote the fact in by hand records it with `edit-and-apply --reason "<what you wrote>"`, no patch needed, or routes the question with `ask`.
6. **A draft's questions wait with it while its lenses are handed off.** In hand-off mode the stage writes the draft, hands its lenses off and reads the draft back from its files once they are in; `feature.yaml` keeps no questions, so the drafter's questions were lost and never raised. The waiting review now keeps them (`review.questions` in `stage/<stage>.yaml`, optional) and raises them when the lenses are in.

No contract shape changes beyond additions: the C3 finding's optional `answered`; `CriticContext.answers` (C4's check context gains a reader); `GateStatus.checkRoundsRun`; the lens output format's optional `answered`; `stage/<stage>.yaml`'s optional `review.questions` and `handedBack`.
